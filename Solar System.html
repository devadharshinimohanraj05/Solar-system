WELCOME TO SOLAR SYSTEM

Let's have a detailed view about the planets in our solar system

The Celestial Bodies

  1. The Sun
  2. The Moon

    The SUN

  1. The Sun is the star at the center of our solar system and the primary driver of life, weather, and space dynamics.
  2. It is a 4.5-billion-year-old yellow dwarf star located at the center of our solar system
  3. Has a radius of about 435,000 miles (700,000 kilometers) and is roughly 109 times wider than Earth
  4. Contains 99.8% of the total mass of the solar system; it would take over 330,000 Earths to match its mass

    The MOON

  1. The Moon is the only natural satellite of Earth
  2. The Moon and Earth are bound by gravitational attraction, which is stronger on the sides facing each other
  3. The Moon is an average of 238,855 miles (384,400 kilometers) away. That means 30 Earth-sized planets could fit in between Earth and the Moon.

THE PLANETS

  1. MERCURY
  2. VENUS
  3. EARTH
  4. MARS
  5. JUPITER
  6. SATURN
  7. URANUS
  8. NEPTUNE

    MERCURY

  1. MERCURY is the smallest planet in our solar system and the closest to the Sun, 
orbiting at an average distance of about 57.9 million kilometers (36 million miles).
  2. It is a rocky terrestrial body with a heavily cratered surface that looks very much like Earth's Moon.

    VENUS

  1. VENUS is the second planet from the Sun and Earth's closest planetary neighbour planet
  2. Surface temperatures reach about 872°F (467°C),
which is hot enough to melt lead. It is the hottest planet in the solar system due to a runaway greenhouse effect.

    EARTH

  1. EARTH is the third planet from the Sun and the only known place in the universe that harbors life
  2. Liquid water covers roughly 70.8% of the surface, earning it the nickname the "Blue Planet"
  3. Earth's atmosphere is primarily made of 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, providing the exact chemical balance needed for life to breathe.

    MARS

  1. MARS is the fourth planet from the Sun and the second-smallest planet in our solar system.
  2.  It gets its iconic reddish hue from a high abundance of iron oxide (rust) covering its rocks and soil

    JUPITER

  1. JUPITER is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest planet in our solar system.
  2. It is a massive gas giant so large that more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it. 
It holds more than twice the total mass of all the other planets in the solar system combined.

    SATURN

  1. SATURN is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest planet in our solar system
  2. It is most famous for its spectacular and complex ring system.
The rings span up to 282,000 kilometres across but are incredibly paper-thin, averaging only about 10 meters (30 feet) thick in most places.
  3.  Saturn's rings are not solid tracks.
They are composed of billions of individual chunks of water ice and rocky debris, ranging in size from microscopic dust grains to objects as large as mountains.

    URANUS

  1. URANUS is the seventh planet from the Sun and the third-largest planet in our solar system.
  2. Uranus has the most unique rotation in the solar system. It is tilted at an extreme 97.77-degree angle,
making it look like it is spinning completely on its side.
  3.  Scientists think a collision with an Earth-sized object long ago knocked it over.

    NEPTUNE

  1. Neptune is the eighth and farthest known planet from the Sun in our solar system.
  2. Neptune is the only planet in our solar system that was discovered using math before it was actually seen.
  3. In 1846, astronomers noticed Uranus's orbit was being pulled unexpectedly,
calculated exactly where an undiscovered planet should be, and pointed their telescopes right at it.
